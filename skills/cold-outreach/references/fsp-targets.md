# FSP Outreach Targets — Role-Specific Angles

Pre-built angles for FSP's most common cold outreach targets. Each section gives the core problem, the FSP value prop framing, a proof point format, and a sample email skeleton.

---

## 1. Insurance Adjusters

**Why they matter:** Independent adjusters and staff adjusters process dozens of claims at a time. When the insured needs a contractor fast, adjusters who know and trust FSP can recommend us — shortening their job and ensuring the scope gets done right the first time.

**Their world:**
- Constantly chasing contractors who miss scopes, delay documentation, or submit incomplete invoices
- Anything that slows the claim cycle is a headache
- They value contractors who document thoroughly (photos, moisture logs, written scope) because it makes their job defensible

**FSP value prop for adjusters:**
- Fast response (same-day site visits for active claims)
- Scope accuracy — we document everything to insurance carrier standards
- Invoice quality — no back-and-forth; first submission is clean and complete
- We work with their timeline, not against it

**Proof point format:**
> "We closed [X] claims last quarter with [insurance carrier]. Average time from first call to invoice submission: [Y] days."

**Subject lines:**
- `south florida claims`
- `contractor for your adjusters`
- `documentation-ready contractor`
- `active claims — quick question`

**Email skeleton:**
> [Name], adjusters dealing with South Florida water claims tell me the biggest headache isn't the scope — it's chasing contractors for documentation.
>
> We're FSP — licensed general contractor in [submarket], specializing in mitigation and remediation. We submit complete invoice packages (photos, moisture logs, itemized scope) on first submission. No back-and-forth.
>
> We've worked [X] claims through [carrier/PA/etc.]. Worth keeping us in your contractor list for South Florida losses?
>
> — Mark

## 2. Property Managers

**Why they matter:** Property managers handle dozens to hundreds of units. Every water damage event, mold call, or storm claim is a problem they need solved fast with minimal tenant disruption. A trusted contractor they can call repeatedly is extremely valuable.

**Their world:**
- Tenant disruption is their biggest fear — a bad mitigation job = tenant complaints + lease risks
- They need fast response (can't have a flooded unit sit)
- They want one vendor who can handle mitigation AND the repair (not separate contractors)
- Insurance claim navigation is confusing — a contractor who helps them through it is a win

**FSP value prop for property managers:**
- Same-day response for active water events
- Full-scope capability: mitigation + remediation + repairs in one contract
- Tenant-aware work: we communicate directly with tenants when authorized, minimize disruption
- We handle the insurance documentation so they don't have to

**Proof point format:**
> "We work with [X] property management companies in South Florida. Average response time for active water events: [Y] hours."

**Subject lines:**
- `water damage — property managers`
- `your next water call`
- `tenant disruption + mitigation`
- `south florida contractor — quick intro`

**Email skeleton:**
> [Name], managing [N+] units in South Florida means eventually fielding a water damage call at the worst time.
>
> We're FSP — licensed contractor specializing in mitigation, remediation, and repairs. We respond same-day to active losses, handle the insurance documentation, and coordinate directly with tenants to minimize disruption.
>
> We work with several property management groups in [submarket]. Worth having us in your contractor list for when it comes up?
>
> — Mark

## 3. Realtors

**Why they matter:** Realtors regularly encounter deals that stall or die because of inspection findings — mold, water damage, moisture intrusion. A contractor who can scope and close those issues fast (pre-listing or mid-transaction) saves the deal. Realtors who trust FSP refer repeatedly.

**Their world:**
- Deals fall apart at inspection over water damage or mold
- Pre-listing inspections flag issues that need fast, credible remediation
- They need a contractor who can provide documentation that satisfies buyers and their agents
- Speed matters — a delayed remediation kills the closing timeline

**FSP value prop for realtors:**
- Fast pre-listing or mid-transaction remediation
- Written scope and completion documentation that satisfies buyers
- Insurance claim support if the seller has coverage
- One contractor handles mitigation + repair — no coordination delays

**Proof point format:**
> "We've helped [X] deals close in South Florida this year where mold or water damage came up at inspection."

**Subject lines:**
- `mold at inspection`
- `deals falling through at inspection`
- `south florida remediation — quick intro`
- `pre-listing mold issues`

**Email skeleton:**
> [Name], curious how often you see deals stall or fall apart because of mold or water damage at inspection.
>
> We're FSP — South Florida contractor specializing in remediation. We can scope and clear most residential mold issues within [X] days and provide written documentation for the buyer's agent. We also handle the insurance claim if the seller has coverage.
>
> We've helped several deals in [submarket] close this year when inspection flagged issues. Worth knowing we exist?
>
> — Mark

## 4. Plumbers & HVAC Technicians

**Why they matter:** Plumbers and HVAC techs are first on scene for the events that cause water damage — burst pipes, water heater failures, AC pan overflows. They regularly find damage that's beyond their scope and need someone to hand off to. A trusted referral partner means better service for their client and a reciprocal lead relationship.

**Their world:**
- They often find water damage while doing their repair — now what?
- They don't want to leave the client without a next step
- They want a contractor they can call who will treat their client right (their reputation is on the line)
- Referral relationships are how they grow — they want it to go both ways

**FSP value prop for plumbers/HVAC:**
- Immediate response when they call from a job site
- We treat their client as a shared client — communication is professional, no undercutting
- Reciprocal referral relationship: we refer back when clients need plumbing or HVAC
- We handle mitigation and remediation cleanly so the client's home is protected

**Proof point format:**
> "We work with [X] plumbers and HVAC companies in South Florida as preferred mitigation partners."

**Subject lines:**
- `mitigation partner — south florida`
- `water damage handoff`
- `referral partnership`
- `when you find water damage`

**Email skeleton:**
> [Name], quick question — when you find water damage on a job, do you have a contractor you trust to hand the client off to?
>
> We're FSP — South Florida remediation contractor. We respond same-day when a plumber or HVAC tech calls us from a job site. We treat your client right (they're your reputation) and we refer back to partners when we encounter plumbing or HVAC needs.
>
> We work with several trade partners in [submarket] this way. Worth a quick call to see if it makes sense?
>
> — Mark

## 5. Public Adjusters (PAs)

**Why they matter:** Public adjusters represent policyholders on insurance claims — they need a contractor who can produce clean scopes, accurate documentation, and invoice packages that hold up under carrier scrutiny. A contractor who makes the PA look good gets repeat business.

**IMPORTANT:** FSP is NOT a public adjuster and never implies otherwise. We partner with PAs — they represent the policyholder on the claim; we do the physical work and documentation.

**Their world:**
- Carriers push back on scopes and invoices that aren't tight
- PAs need contractors whose documentation is airtight — photos, moisture logs, line-item scopes
- They need contractors who show up when they say they will
- A bad contractor can undermine a good claim

**FSP value prop for PAs:**
- Documentation that holds up: photos, moisture readings, written scope to carrier standards
- Responsive communication — we update the PA as the job progresses
- Invoice quality — first submission is clean, reducing carrier back-and-forth
- We understand the PA/carrier dynamic and don't step on the PA's role

**Subject lines:**
- `documentation-ready contractor`
- `south florida mitigation — pa partnership`
- `carrier-standard scopes`
- `quick intro — mitigation contractor`

**Email skeleton:**
> [Name], PAs tell me their biggest contractor headache is chasing documentation after the job — or dealing with carrier pushback on a scope that wasn't written tightly.
>
> We're FSP — licensed South Florida contractor. We produce carrier-standard documentation (photos, moisture logs, itemized scopes) on every job. We work alongside PAs — your role, our physical work, clean paper trail.
>
> We're active in [submarket]. Worth a quick call to see if there's a fit?
>
> — Mark

## Personalization Signals to Watch For

For any target type, these signals make the email significantly stronger:

| Signal | How to use it |
|--------|--------------|
| Recent hurricane/storm activity | "With [storm name] damage still being assessed in [area]…" |
| Adjuster/PA handling a big loss | "Heard [development/area] got hit hard — assuming you're busy right now" |
| Realtor active in a specific neighborhood | "Noticed you've been active in [area] — lots of older inventory there with deferred maintenance" |
| Property manager with new listings/units | "Saw you picked up [property] — congrats. Water events in multi-family are… frequent." |
| Plumber/HVAC expanding or hiring | "Noticed you're growing the team — probably means more job site calls and more referral volume" |

## What FSP Never Says in Outreach

Per fsp-CLAUDE.md brand rules:

- Never claim to be or work as a public adjuster
- Never quote insurance settlement amounts or coverage interpretations
- Never mention competitors by name
- Never promise timelines we can't control
- No "synergy," "partnership opportunity," "best-in-class," or corporate boilerplate
- Never open with "I hope this email finds you well"
